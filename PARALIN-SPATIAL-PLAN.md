# Paralin Spatial Plan

> **Status:** Working spatial canon for the Storypath rebuild  
> **Updated:** 6 October 2026  
> **Identity source:** `STORYPATH-WORLD-IDENTITY.md`  
> **Curriculum source:** `STANDARD-3-MATHEMATICS-TOWN-LEARNING-JOURNEY.md`

## Purpose

This document defines the spatial logic beneath Paralin's current visual direction. It does **not** turn the 18 Mathematics stops into 18 isolated buildings. Paralin must remain a believable, interconnected Caribbean community in which mathematical needs emerge naturally from everyday places, routes, services, events, residents, and environmental systems.

The current Paralin visual concept establishes the preferred overall composition: lush hills behind the community, a dense civic/commercial centre, connected roads, recreation and creative spaces, a river/waterfall system, and a coastline.

## Core spatial principle

**The world is the interface.**

Locations should be recognizable through the environment itself. Production maps should avoid permanent floating location icons and labels. On pointer devices, a location may reveal its identity through hover/focus; on touch devices, first tap may reveal identity and a subsequent action may enter. Accessible names and keyboard focus must remain available regardless of visual treatment.

## Paralin zones

The zones below are design/planning regions, not necessarily child-facing district names.

### I. Paralin Centre

Dense everyday commercial and community activity around the main road.

Supports:
- Stop 2 — Harbour Storehouse / Supply Ledger function
- Stop 5 — Corner Shop Challenge
- Stop 7 — Big Bakehouse
- Stop 13 — Farmers' Market

The centre should visibly exchange goods and people with the delivery depot, maxi route, community events, and surrounding neighbourhoods.

### II. Civic and School Area

A walkable civic cluster connected to the centre and residential streets.

Supports:
- Stop 1 — Grand Library Book Hunt / Community Register function
- Stop 4 — Parcel Post Mystery
- teaching/guided-learning spaces where appropriate
- community-centre functions that may support later events

The library, post/parcel service, school, and community facilities should feel like parts of one functioning community rather than curriculum buildings.

### III. Makers Quarter

A practical creative/working area where making, repair, fabric, construction, pattern, and cultural production overlap.

Supports:
- Stop 3 — Kite-Maker's Yard
- Stop 8 — Tailor's Loft
- Stop 9 — Builders' Yard
- Stop 10 — Mas Camp

These places should share residents, materials, deliveries, and story consequences. They may be visually distinct while belonging to the same working ecosystem.

### IV. Savannah and Community Grounds

Large flexible recreation and event space.

Supports:
- Stop 12 — Playground Project
- Stop 15 — Sports Day at the Savannah
- Stop 18 — Grand Community Fair, in combination with Paralin Centre

This area should visibly change over time. The playground project can alter the grounds, Sports Day can temporarily reorganize them, and the final community fair can transform them.

### V. River and Coast

The river system runs from the upper hills through/along Paralin to the coast. Wetland and coastal areas support environmental inquiry and community movement.

Supports:
- Stop 16 — Mangrove Count
- Stop 17 — Community Radio Station near the river/central edge
- environmental and data-transfer opportunities

Data gathered in the mangrove experience should be capable of travelling narratively into the radio-station experience.

### VI. Paralin Heights

Greener upper community and hillside terrain connected by winding roads and the river/waterfall system.

Supports:
- Stop 3 may extend into this area where kite-making/flying benefits from open elevation
- Stop 14 — Water Park Adventure / water-and-container function
- nature, water, route, and later transfer experiences

The visual character may quietly draw from lush inland Trinidad landscapes without reproducing a real place.

## The connective spine

Paralin's main road is a major gameplay asset, not decorative scenery.

It connects the commercial centre, transport/delivery activity, civic area, community grounds, river crossing, residential branches, and roads into the hills.

### Stop 11 — Maxi Route Adventure

Stop 11 uses **Paralin's road network itself** rather than requiring a dedicated attraction.

The route system supports:
- kilometres, metres, and centimetres;
- unit choice;
- distance estimation;
- measurement and conversion;
- repeated intervals;
- route and distance problems.

Maxis, deliveries, residents, and earlier/later missions can reuse the same road network so movement through Paralin accumulates meaning.

## Stop 18 rule

The Grand Community Fair is **not an eighteenth permanent building**.

It is the payoff for learning Paralin.

The community transforms:
- temporary stalls and structures appear;
- residents from earlier experiences return;
- the Bakehouse, Market, Mas Camp, school, builders, transport network, radio station, and other community systems participate;
- previous actions and evidence can be visible;
- the learner independently selects relevant Mathematics from across the journey.

The final experience should make the player feel that they know this community.

## Relationship examples

Paralin should support cross-location continuity such as:

- Farmers' Market → Bakehouse: produce becomes ingredients.
- Delivery Depot → Corner Shop / Market / Bakehouse: logistics connect businesses.
- Builders' Yard → Playground Project: an earlier relationship returns with a new mathematical need.
- Mangrove Count → Radio Station: learner-collected data becomes information that must be represented and communicated.
- Earlier community work → Grand Community Fair: residents, objects, improvements, records, and evidence return.

These connections are examples of the system, not the complete story script.

## Progression and discovery

Do not rely on giant padlocks over the world.

A location may physically exist before the learner has a reason to enter it. Story progression can create relevance, invitations, changing activity, new routes, resident requests, or environmental cues.

The player's early mental map may be small — for example home/school/library/corner shop — and expand as Paralin becomes meaningful.

## Visual interaction rules

- No permanent floating hotspot icons across the map.
- No permanent location-label pills covering the environment.
- The building, route, landscape, vehicle, or activity should communicate the hotspot whenever possible.
- Hover/focus may subtly animate, brighten, lift, outline, or otherwise respond.
- A restrained Storypath name treatment may appear only when the location is hovered, focused, selected, or otherwise contextually relevant.
- Touch interactions must not depend on hover.
- Locked/future experiences should remain part of the world rather than becoming visually dominated by lock graphics.
- Essential interaction text and controls remain dynamic accessible UI, never baked into the raster environment.

## Visual concept status

The current clean Paralin environment mockup is a **visual and composition reference**, not final production art.

Preserve from it:
- elevated illustrated-game perspective;
- lush hills and waterfall;
- coastline;
- dense connected community;
- road hierarchy;
- civic/commercial centre;
- sports/creative areas;
- river crossing;
- warm, lively Caribbean atmosphere;
- environmental storytelling and visible community activity.

Do not canonize incidental generated details simply because they appear in the concept image.

Future Paralin environment concepts should use this spatial plan to ensure all 18 Mathematics experiences have believable homes.
