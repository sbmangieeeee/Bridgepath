# Storypath Character Art Direction

## Status
**Production Character Style — Approved**

Aaliyah and Theo are the canonical visual references for the Storypath character system. All future Storypath characters should use the same visual language, rendering approach, proportions, lighting, level of stylization, and production quality.

The goal is not for every character to resemble Aaliyah or Theo physically. The goal is for every character to feel as though they were designed by the same art team for the same world.

## 1. Character Art Vision
Storypath uses a polished, expressive contemporary 2D/2.5D mobile-game character style. Characters should feel warm, expressive, approachable, Caribbean, contemporary, highly readable, animation-friendly, slightly exaggerated, and dimensional without becoming photorealistic.

The direction takes cues from premium casual narrative/adventure games in character readability, expressive proportions, smooth forms, dimensional shading, and animation friendliness, while Storypath retains original character identities, Caribbean context, costumes, environments, colour language, and world design.

> **Illustrated enough to feel magical, dimensional enough to feel alive, and simplified enough to function as a game character.**

## 2. Canonical Character References
### Aaliyah
Aaliyah establishes the core Storypath character language: stylized preteen proportions, moderately enlarged head for gameplay readability, expressive but believable eyes, rounded facial construction, simplified anatomy, clean silhouette, soft dimensional rendering, controlled facial detail, natural Black hair representation, rich deep-brown skin rendering, simplified animation-friendly clothing, and expressive but restrained game-character proportions.

Her defining silhouette includes large natural twin afro puffs, burgundy bows, glasses, and the burgundy Paralin Primary pinafore. Her hair should read as soft, dense natural texture and mass rather than thousands of individually defined curls.

### Theo
Theo establishes the corresponding male preteen character language using the same rendering system, facial abstraction, lighting, anatomy simplification, clothing treatment, animation readiness, colour treatment, and surface finish.

Theo is slightly taller than Aaliyah. His defining silhouette includes a short curly top with tapered/faded sides, cream school shirt, burgundy school shorts, white socks, and black school shoes. His curls are looser than Aaliyah's texture and remain relatively short on top.

Aaliyah and Theo must always appear to belong to the **same character universe**.

## 3. Character Proportions
Storypath characters are stylized rather than anatomically photorealistic. For preteen characters:
- Heads are moderately enlarged for gameplay readability.
- Eyes may be slightly enlarged but remain believable.
- Facial features are simplified into strong graphic forms.
- Torsos are somewhat compact.
- Limbs remain naturally proportioned.
- Hands are simplified but expressive.
- Feet/shoes may be slightly emphasized for silhouette stability.
- Characters must not become chibi or toddler-like.

Variation in height, build, face shape, hair, complexion, posture, and features is encouraged, while maintaining the same **degree of stylization**.

## 4. Faces
Characters should have recognizable individual facial structures rather than a generic template. Preserve variation in face width, cheek structure, jaw shape, nose, eyes, eyebrows, lips, smiles, and ears.

Eyes should communicate emotion clearly during gameplay without becoming anime/chibi-sized. Skin should have dimensional warmth and subtle variation without photorealistic pores or excessive surface texture.

## 5. Black and Caribbean Hair
Hair is a major component of Storypath identity and silhouette. Natural Black and Caribbean hairstyles should be represented intentionally and accurately, including afro puffs, afros, twists, braids, cornrows, locs, curls, coils, fades, tapered cuts, natural loose hair, and protective styles.

Do not default all Black characters to one curl pattern. Prioritize:
1. overall silhouette
2. mass and volume
3. texture family
4. major curl/coil groups
5. selected detail

Avoid rendering every strand individually. At gameplay scale, the hairstyle should remain recognizable primarily from silhouette.

## 6. Skin Tone
Storypath represents the broad range of complexions found throughout the Caribbean. Do not use one generic brown tone. Skin may range naturally from deep and dark browns through medium, warm, golden, and lighter complexions, with varied undertones.

Skin must retain richness under Storypath's warm lighting. Do not unintentionally lighten darker-skinned characters through excessive highlights. Aaliyah's deeper complexion is intentional; Theo's complexion is intentionally lighter than Aaliyah's.

## 7. Rendering
Use polished illustrated rendering with clean silhouettes, smooth forms, soft dimensional shading, subtle cel/painterly characteristics, controlled highlights, warm lighting, rich colour, restrained surface texture, and strong facial readability.

Avoid photorealism, hyperrealism, pixel art, flat vector characters, anime, chibi, clay characters, plastic-toy rendering, excessively realistic anatomy, and extreme caricature.

## 8. Clothing
Clothing should be simple enough for animation while retaining believable construction. Prioritize clear silhouettes, large readable shapes, limited unnecessary seams, restrained folds, consistent colour blocks, and repeatable costume construction.

## 9. Paralin Primary Uniform
Primary school colour: **Mahogany / Burgundy**.

**Aaliyah:** cream short-sleeved collared blouse; burgundy pleated pinafore; white knee socks; black Mary Jane shoes; burgundy hair bows.

**Theo:** cream short-sleeved collared shirt; burgundy tailored shorts; dark belt; white knee socks; black school shoes.

The Paralin Primary crest should be treated as a reusable game asset rather than baked inconsistently into generated characters.

## 10. Silhouette Design
Important recurring characters should be recognizable from silhouette alone through hairstyle, height, proportions, clothing, accessories, and posture.

Aaliyah: enormous twin natural puffs + bows + pinafore + glasses.
Theo: taller lean silhouette + short curly top/fade + shorts.

New characters should not simply reproduce these silhouettes.

## 11. Animation Readiness
Initial production references should be full-body, front-facing, neutral stance, arms slightly separated from torso, legs clearly separated, hands visible, feet visible, and with minimal perspective distortion.

Production sequence:
1. Front Reference
2. Side View
3. Back View
4. 3/4 View
5. Expression Sheet
6. Idle
7. Walk
8. Run
9. Talk
10. Think
11. Interact
12. Celebrate

Additional animations should be created only when gameplay requires them. Storypath is not primarily a combat game; attack, damage, and death are not part of the default character animation library.

## 12. Expression Language
Important expressions include neutral, happy, curious, thinking, confused, surprised, excited, proud, concerned, determined, and celebrating. Expressions must remain readable at mobile gameplay scale without unnecessary theatrical exaggeration.

## 13. Character Diversity
Paralin should feel like a believable Caribbean community rather than repeated templates. Vary complexion, hair texture/style, facial structure, height, body type, age, clothing, personality, and cultural background. Characters should look related to the same **world**, not necessarily to each other.

## 14. Character / Environment Relationship
Characters and environments must share the same visual universe. Environments may contain more detail, but characters must not appear pasted onto realistic backgrounds. Keep colour saturation, lighting, softness, shape simplification, dimensionality, and illustrative treatment compatible.

> **Character + environment = one illustrated game world.**

## 15. Gameplay Readability
Storypath is primarily experienced at gameplay scale. **Silhouette beats micro-detail.** Hair shape, costume colour, body language, facial expression, and major accessories must communicate before fine texture.

## 16. AI Generation Rules
AI references have separate responsibilities.

**Identity reference determines:** facial structure, complexion, hair characteristics, distinctive physical traits.

**Storypath style reference determines:** degree of stylization, proportions, rendering, lighting, eye treatment, anatomy simplification, clothing treatment, game readability.

> **Identity determines WHO the character is. Storypath art direction determines HOW that person is translated into the game.**

Do not allow a generation model to redesign identity simply to fit a generic cartoon template.

## 17. Consistency Test
Before approving a recurring character, place them beside Aaliyah and Theo and ask:

> **If the names were removed, would these characters immediately look like they were created by the same studio for the same game?**

Compare head-to-body ratio, eye treatment, facial abstraction, anatomy, hands, feet, lighting, shading, texture, clothing detail, colour treatment, and overall gamification. If one looks significantly more realistic, cartoonish, flat, detailed, or younger, it requires another style-matching pass.

## 18. Canonical Rule
Aaliyah and Theo are the initial **Storypath Character Style Masters**. Future characters reference them for visual consistency.

**Do not repeatedly regenerate Aaliyah or Theo to accommodate new characters.** New characters conform to the established Storypath system. The style masters change only if the overall Storypath art direction is intentionally revised.

## Summary
> **Storypath characters are warm, expressive, stylized Caribbean characters rendered in a polished dimensional 2D/2.5D mobile-game style, with strong silhouettes, authentic individual features, natural diversity, animation-friendly construction, and enough exaggeration to feel playful without losing believable human identity.**

Aaliyah and Theo define that standard.
